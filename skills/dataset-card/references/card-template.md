# Data card guide

Start with a broad reconnaissance card, even with no seed. Cover the dimensions below using documentation and representative inspection; deepen them when candidates appear. Record unknowns without waiting to fill every cell.

## Identity and scope

Name the dataset, version, collection purpose, input paths, authorized planning subset and excluded data. Label facts as documented, code-inferred, observed, owner-reported or unknown, with their source. Record which outcome summaries have been inspected for planning.

## Measurements and units

Map the major variable families: meaning, units, timing, resolution, missing-value conventions and availability. Distinguish rows, repeated measurements, nested groups and independent units using collection documentation.

## Design and acquisition

Describe conditions, assignment, sampling, repeated observations, temporal structure, interventions and selection rules. Include behavioral counts and marginal distributions when authorized. Explain how each diagnostic informs coverage or measurability rather than testing a candidate effect.

## Coverage and co-measurement

Inspect representative units, modalities and conditions. Record counts, joint availability, matching keys and simultaneous measurements with denominators and sample scope. Verify key multiplicity, common clocks and valid simultaneous windows at the required unit; common file keys alone do not establish usable joint observations. Link operational definitions and relevant local-version corrections. Expand coverage where it could change the research direction.

## Design-dissociation table

| Distinction suggested by the design | Separating condition or observation | Evidence and coverage | Remaining ambiguity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

Build initial rows from meaningful design contrasts before selecting questions. For example, conditions collected only in different periods may be inseparable from collection period; repeated measurements within each period could provide a useful comparison. Outcome counts may establish that a comparison is represented, while effect estimation is a later analysis.

## Unusual opportunities and limitations

Describe distinctive combinations, variation, repetition or timing, and the types of scientific questions they might enable. Connect each opportunity to an observed or documented feature. Distinguish confirmed absence from uninspected content and identify the smallest useful next check.

## Candidate-directed updates

For each candidate, link the necessary measurements, joins, independent units and comparison structure to existing evidence. Resolve important unknowns with local checks or owner questions, then record whether the finding supports, changes or prevents the proposed use.

## Checks and continuation

Link scripts and outputs with input scope, date and limitations. Save enough context to resume from the card and its evidence. Keep credentials and raw records out of the card.
