# Make critique change the research

Generate provisional ideas freely; check consequential premises before investing heavily in them. Use independent host-native contexts when available and authorized, keeping their combined work inside the research budget. Assign a bounded scientific decision and its evidence, not one reviewer per file or a standing set of roles. A small task can combine checks. Use the host's available agents without requiring a new provider or API route; record what context and evidence they actually received and could inspect.

## Check foundations and inference at the point of use

Before choosing the main directions for deep development, have an independent context inspect the foundation those directions depend on: measurement and design meaning; relevant theories and experimental contrasts; any claimed source-to-target paper transfer; and the closest originating/reuse work, including unresolved close leads. Give the actual source notes and authorized evidence, not just the candidate's summary of them. Look beyond the candidate list for a consequential missing theory, experiment or connection to another topic. Check which premise or omission could change the investment decision against primary sources or permitted data, including whether a proposed theory prediction is actually implied, and repair the affected foundation note and candidates. This is a focused check of consequential dependencies, not a literature quota or a requirement to finish every background file before generating ideas.

Before elaborating a complicated estimator, challenge the proposed inference: can the stated observations distinguish the explanation or target quantity claimed, under what assumptions, and what counterexample would defeat it? A short derivation or source check may decide this more cheaply than implementation. Inspect scientific payoff as well as answerability. Narrowing a claim must leave something worth learning; a feasible computation alone does not justify further work.

For an initial independent assessment, provide the exact question and claim, sources, data documentation, permitted access, checks and outputs, assumptions and exposure/scope limits. Withhold earlier reviewer reports and author self-ratings or desired verdicts. A new context may inherit project instructions or encounter review conclusions embedded in the dossier: disclose that exposure instead of claiming blindness. A focused initial request can read:

> Assess [premise or proposed claim] in [artifact/revision] against [authorized sources and checks], within [scope and budget]. Identify decision-changing defects and the smallest check that could resolve them. Check false rejection as well as overclaiming; report evidence and access limits, not a vote on the author's preferred outcome.

Failure under one proxy does not establish that all scientifically justified definitions fail.

## Repair, verify and stop

At the start, record a finite repair allowance in `brief.md`, within the task's total time/usage budget and with capacity reserved for final checking and delivery. Unless the task sets another limit, allow at most **two substantive author-repair/verification cycles per candidate or linked scientific argument**, after its initial assessment. Each cycle runs from an author repair and its evidence through re-review: an initial assessment plus two completed cycles can require three reviewer interactions. A new filename, reviewer or reframe does not reset that allowance. Final-document repairs consume the same allowance; editorial corrections need not start a scientific cycle.

Keep a concise record in `review.md` for each consequential issue: the claim and reviewed artifact/revision; the objection; the deciding check and linked evidence; the author's change or evidence-backed disagreement; verification of the repaired revision; and what remains unresolved. Count completed repair-and-verification cycles from their actual interventions and record the stopping reason here. Carry that same reason into the brief, dossier and NEXT: an external-information block differs from an exhausted allowance. Plain Markdown suffices; do not create a machine state, role registry or transcript archive.

Complete a cycle as follows:

1. **Check the objection and proposed remedy.** Trace disputed meaning to collection/processing evidence or an identifying argument; reproducing a count does not establish its meaning. A reviewer's suggested fix is unverified. Check its assumptions and effect on the supported claim before adopting it, and correct reviewer mistakes explicitly. Choose a source check, derivation, counterexample, authorized structural probe or synthetic diagnostic as appropriate. Where a computation needs validation, test the actual proposed rule and consequential combinations, including a relevant failure case; a passing simpler proxy does not validate the replacement.
2. **Repair the affected work.** Revise the question, premise, calculation or interpretation and the dependent claims in the dossier and current notes. After a material reframe, renew affected literature, value and data checks. Mark superseded conclusions so continuation does not revive them. If the objection does not apply, retain the specific evidence for that judgment; if necessary evidence is unavailable, retain the condition rather than filling it with a default.
3. **Verify the repair independently.** Give a reviewer the specific finding, changed passages or artifacts with their revisions, check inputs/outputs and the claims affected. Start by assessing the resulting claim and its evidence, then reconcile the earlier finding and change list. If the repair introduced a decision or interpretation rule, state the implication it relies on and test a case that could make that implication fail, using a short derivation or an appropriate executable check of the actual rule. Report what was verified, what failed and what remains unverified; confirming that the requested edit was made does not close its scientific validity. This focused pass can reuse a reviewer; it is not a blind first assessment. Collect the finding on the repaired work before marking the issue resolved. A promise to test later, a written author response or a review of the previous version is not repair verification.

Before delegating, check how the host returns completion and findings. Use supported completion notification, wait/result collection or synchronous mode, and collect the actual status and findings, including linked result files. File presence, headings and stable size alone do not establish completion.

Stop a line earlier if review brings no new decision-relevant evidence, necessary facts cannot be obtained within scope/budget, or the remaining scientific payoff no longer warrants development. Stop at its repair allowance or the task budget even if objections remain. Record the unresolved claim and smallest useful next check; preserve unaffected supported claims, and label the rest conditional, review pending or rejected only as the evidence warrants. Exhaustion alone is not scientific rejection. At the cap, remove overclaims or correct status, but do not introduce an unchecked replacement procedure or deciding rule. An unverified rule that determines a verdict remains an unresolved decision in `NEXT.md`: make the next action establish and check that rule before applying it, rather than supply it as a tentative default. Do not relabel exhaustion as acceptance, start another cycle by renaming the same argument, or keep narrowing solely to obtain a passing verdict. A genuinely different opportunity may be considered only within the remaining task budget. Do not run a real target analysis to make a check pass; synthetic success establishes only the properties tested under its assumptions.

## Check the finished document

Prepare `recommendations.md`, `NEXT.md` and the intended user-facing summary before the final independent check. The summary can be kept in `review.md`; no extra artifact is required. Give the reviewer these exact versions and linked, authorized evidence, without relying on the author's conversational explanation. Earlier candidate review does not cover newly written computation or conclusions. Combine the following in one pass when practical, or divide distinct uncertainties for a substantial dossier:

- **Evidence and claims:** follow the central question, closest-precedent comparison, decisive data facts, computations and interpretations. Check citations against their sources. When a derived measurement determines either support or rejection, trace it from permitted source records through the calculation to the reported output, and independently check a case that could break its meaning. Match a reported count to its actual predicates: failing a compound eligibility rule does not show which component failed. Check whether normalization, alignment or filtering really removes the alternative explanation claimed. Use the smallest appropriate source, algebraic or executable check of relevant units, joins, indexing, transformations and boundaries; agreement with the saved output alone is insufficient. If the necessary check cannot be done within scope or budget, name that measurement as unverified. Check the delivered question and all newly introduced rules, not just whether earlier objections were answered. Name unsupported claims, hidden assumptions and contradictions at their locations, with the source or counterexample that makes them consequential.
- **Readability:** read in the user's language as a scientific colleague new to the task. Check that the text develops the question, its importance, the proposed comparison and plausible interpretations in a coherent account. Replace repetitive disclaimers and defensive contrasts with a direct explanation; keep each consequential qualification beside the claim or calculation it changes. Suggest specific repairs, preserving supported status and all decisions needed for implementation.
- **Implementation handoff:** follow [the handoff check](implementation-handoff.md). Determine whether the proposed computation can be translated without inventing scientific choices. Code is useful only when it exposes or tests a consequential gap.
- **Delivery consistency:** compare the report, continuation request, prepared summary and the working notes that NEXT asks a later session to load. They must identify the same supported and conditional claims, review limitations, prior exposure and next authorized action. Where a linked candidate or paper-transfer note retains a superseded conclusion, mark that conclusion and point to the supported revision. The summary cannot claim acceptance or novelty beyond the report; `NEXT.md` cannot silently authorize target execution or revive a discarded design.

Record the exact reviewed paths and revisions (or hashes), prepared summary text and collected findings in `review.md`. Apply the repair/verification cycle above to substantive changes. Before sending, compare the deliverables with the checked versions; recheck any subsequent scientific change, including a suggested fix from this final reviewer. If capacity is exhausted or independent checking is unavailable, deliver the useful dossier with affected claims and final review/handoff explicitly pending. An author check can catch errors but cannot supply missing independent verification. Keep the final message a short index: the supported or conditional disposition, the main question in plain language, and links to the checked dossier and NEXT. Reuse that prepared text, changing only links and presentation; leave detailed scientific claims and diagnostics in the dossier. If the final disposition has been downgraded, update the affected status and limitation directly. A substantive rewrite is new unreviewed text and uses the same remaining allowance; do not backfill acceptance later.
