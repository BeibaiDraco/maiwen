# Choose which questions deserve deeper planning

Use a fresh context separate from the questioner. Read candidates, `early-ideas.md`, the three substantive knowledge sources and relevant preparation findings. Treat generated and early ideas on the same scientific criteria, preserving their origins. Use [question development](question-development.md) to judge what an answer would change; descriptive, predictive and replication questions can have value without a mechanism contest.

For each promising family, state its closest actual comparison and the remaining contribution. Before developing or rejecting it because a paper did not do something, reopen the primary figure, methods, derivation or code that could erase that difference. Use [prior-use](../../prior-use/SKILL.md) for conceptual equivalents and follow-up work. Record what was compared, what was found and the precise proposed change; explain how that evidence supports the decision. An inaccessible decisive passage leaves that claim unresolved, rather than proving novelty or duplication.

Check overlap and obvious measurement/design barriers. Preserve distinct field opportunities omitted by generation when they warrant consideration; avoid creating cosmetic variants to fill a quota. Distinguish a demonstrated obstacle from feasibility that remains unexamined.

Write `screening.md` with evidence-backed reasons to develop, combine, revise, reject or defer. Distinguish information and capacity deferrals from scientific rejection. For each shortlisted question, identify the first uncertainty worth resolving before major investment. Size deep planning to remaining time and review capacity rather than selecting a fixed number of elaborate plans. A planner's deciding result can return an opportunity to the queue or close it; changing the label does not reset its repair allowance.

For an additional batch, compare its scientific arguments with the earlier candidates and failure evidence. Identify genuinely different opportunities versus old arguments under new wording; carry consumed repair cycles for the latter.

Return current source notes, the shortlist and useful queued alternatives to the coordinator. Do not edit the original candidate list or launch planners.
