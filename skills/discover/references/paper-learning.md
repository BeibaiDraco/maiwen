# Learn how a paper made its question answerable

## Find useful studies

If the user supplies PDFs, make a short index of titles, citations and local paths, then read the relevant question, design, methods, figures and limitations with the host's available PDF tools. Check tables, equations and captions against the PDF when extraction loses structure. If a file cannot be read, record the limitation and find an accessible primary version; do not infer its methods from a title or pretend to have read it.

With or without local papers, use the [field opportunity map](field-grounding.md) to search shared scientific problems across systems, tasks and topics, alongside nearby and later work. Follow a theoretical prediction, unresolved disagreement or revealing experimental comparison when it opens a new way to ask about the data. Select studies for that reasoning, including sources beyond the dataset papers' citation neighborhood. Record the useful connection and its effect on the question; shift effort from collecting cases to developing candidates when further reading adds little.

Keep local PDFs in place. Save short notes in `research/<dataset>/paperbank/`, with a citation, link or local path and page/figure/section locators. Distinguish full-text reading from abstracts, secondary summaries and unavailable passages. Use only necessary short excerpts. User papers can be private: do not upload their contents or distinctive private text to external services without permission; search public concepts instead.

## Reconstruct the question-forming move

For a case worth using, explain in ordinary prose:

- What was puzzling or missing, and why would its resolution matter?
- Which measurement, comparison, variation or redefinition turned that concern into a tractable question?
- How does the comparison support the inference? Which conditions are essential, which strengthen it, and which are incidental details?
- What would different plausible results mean, and which explanations remain possible?
- What reasoning move might transfer, and where would it fail?

For a transfer that determines a candidate, reopen the decisive result and methods: identify the actual population, comparison and inference it supports, with a figure, equation or passage locator. Separate the source's conclusion from your proposed extension, then try a concrete counterexample to that extension. Preserve selection conditions and distinct mechanisms rather than merging them under a convenient label. Label your reconstruction as an interpretation; do not invent the authors' historical thought process. Keep necessary conditions even when they are dataset-specific in the source. A sample size used in one paper is not a universal threshold.

## Use the case to change a question

For a candidate credited to paper learning, write or link its actual mapping: source feature X had inferential role R; target feature Y may or may not perform R; the missing condition is Z; therefore ask or revise question Q. Verify Y and Z against the target data. State whether the case opened a question, changed its comparison or ruled out a transfer. Label other origins as field questions or data opportunities rather than attaching an ornamental paper citation.

When essential conditions are absent, either establish a different defensible comparison with its own value or stop that transfer. A rejected transfer can help define a better direction. Link the changed candidate to the case and the decisive evidence; a citation without a consequential change does not show that paper learning helped.

For a useful question-forming move, save a short pattern with the originating scientific tension, the comparison that resolves it, necessary conditions and failure cases. Link its supporting case(s); distinguish a single-case transfer hypothesis from a pattern supported across studies. For each pattern used, show the candidate it opened or changed and the verified target-data correspondence. If several cases share a move, consolidate them without erasing differences in their inferential conditions. A pattern suggests questions; it grants no novelty or feasibility. Optional curated domain materials and legacy case notes must be checked against their sources before relying on them. If no useful transfer emerges, continue from field and data opportunities and say so.
