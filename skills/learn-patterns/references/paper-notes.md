# Explain a paper well enough to reason from it

Write detailed English notes for papers that determine field understanding, pattern learning, prior-use judgments or a candidate's fate. Keep a compact index for peripheral search leads. Scale detail to the scientific role, not a word-count target; a new reader should be able to reconstruct the argument without relying on the note author's conversation.

Identify the paper, stable public locator or authorized local source, reading depth and relevant pages, figures, tables, equations or methods sections. Explain in connected prose:

1. **Problem and stakes.** What was unknown or disputed, what prior work established, and why the unresolved distinction mattered.
2. **Data and design.** Population or system, task, manipulation or observational structure, measured variables, observation units and pertinent selection. For dataset reuse, identify actual collection/version/subset evidence separately from mere citation.
3. **Decisive comparison.** What the researchers compared, measured or derived, which alternatives it could distinguish and under which assumptions. Describe important computational steps rather than only method names.
4. **Findings and inference.** What was observed, which conclusion follows and its source locator. Distinguish reported results, author interpretation and your reconstruction. Reopen decisive source passages before attributing a strong claim.
5. **Remaining uncertainty.** Alternative explanations, limitations, contradictory evidence and later work that changes the account. An old discussion section is not proof a question remains open today.
6. **Use here.** How this understanding changes the field map, a pattern, a prior-use judgment or an opportunity. Keep the source conclusion distinct from your proposed extension.

Explain question formation and hypothesis formation separately where the paper supports them: what unknown became worth asking, and how a proposed answer generated a testable prediction. They may develop together; a descriptive question need not supply a mechanistic explanation. A hypothesis is a proposed answer rather than an observed result or a method name. Mark your extensions and distinguish evidence motivating a hypothesis from evidence that tests it.

For theoretical papers, explain the model's explanatory aim, assumptions, predicted observable and relevant derivation. Separate a computational principle from a particular implementation or physical location. For empirical papers, explain the actual measurement and design contrast. A review can orient either account; verify consequential claims in the primary source where available.

Use English explanations and translated titles where necessary, preserving stable identifiers and scientific notation. Do not modify original source documents or raw data. Save only necessary short quotations with attribution; keep PDFs in their authorized location. An abstract-only source gets an honest short account of what was accessible and what remains unknown, never an invented full-methods explanation.

Reuse existing substantive notes by linking them from different knowledge streams. If a shared note changes, tell the coordinator which judgments depend on it so affected candidates and plans can be updated. File presence, length and citation count alone do not establish understanding.
