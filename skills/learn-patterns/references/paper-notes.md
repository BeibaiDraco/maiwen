# Explain a paper well enough to reason from it

Write substantive English notes for papers that shape the field account, pattern library, prior-use judgment or a candidate question. A scientist new to the project should understand the study's argument and how it informs this work. Give peripheral search leads a short index entry; give consequential sources the detail their role requires.

Identify the paper, its stable public link or authorized local location, the parts read and the relevant pages, figures, equations or methods. Develop the explanation in connected prose:

1. **The problem.** Explain what earlier work established, what remained uncertain or disputed, and why resolving it mattered.
2. **The observations.** Describe the population or system, task, intervention or observational design, measured variables, observation units and important selection. If the paper reused this dataset, identify the collection, version or subset it actually used.
3. **The comparison.** Explain what the researchers compared, measured or derived and why it addressed the question. Describe the important computational steps and the assumptions needed to interpret them.
4. **The finding.** State what the study found and what conclusion the evidence supports, with a source locator. Identify the authors' interpretation and any additional inference of your own. Reopen the relevant passage before attributing a consequential claim.
5. **What remains open.** Explain alternative accounts, limitations, conflicting evidence and later work that changes the interpretation. Check whether subsequent studies have answered a question raised in the discussion.
6. **Its use in this project.** Explain how the paper changes the field account, a question pattern, a prior-use judgment or a research opportunity. Identify extensions you propose beyond the paper's own conclusions.

Where the source allows, reconstruct question formation and hypothesis formation separately. What unknown became worth asking? What proposed answer or explanation led to an observable prediction? Explain how they informed each other, and distinguish evidence that motivated a hypothesis from evidence that tested it. A descriptive study may contribute a useful question without proposing a mechanism.

For a theoretical paper, explain the model's purpose, assumptions, relevant derivation and predicted observation. Identify whether it describes a computational principle, a particular implementation or a physical location. For an empirical paper, explain the measurement and design comparison. Reviews can orient either account; check the primary sources for claims that determine a scientific judgment.

Use English explanations and translated titles where needed, preserving identifiers and notation. Keep source documents unchanged and PDFs in their authorized location; save only necessary short quotations with attribution. If only an abstract is available, describe what it establishes and which methods or results still need reading.

Link existing substantive notes from other knowledge accounts when useful. When a shared note changes, tell the coordinator which judgments depend on it so the affected questions and plans can be updated.
