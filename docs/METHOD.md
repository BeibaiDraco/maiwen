# Method and lineage

A dataset offers measurements, not a ready-made scientific agenda. Maiwen aims to develop questions that are worth answering and that the available observations can support. These are separate demands: an executable calculation can be scientifically redundant, and an important question can be unidentifiable in a particular design.

## Three inputs to question formation

**Field knowledge** describes what scientists are trying to understand. A researcher reads relevant experiments, reviews and theoretical work, including neighboring subfields with useful ideas. The account explains contested interpretations and open problems, rather than merely listing paper titles. Theory contributes predictions only when its assumptions and proposed observables are made explicit.

**Question patterns** explain how strong studies made progress. The bank reconstructs the scientific tension, the decisive comparison and the data conditions that made it informative. It distinguishes forming a question from proposing a hypothesis and deriving predictions. A descriptive or predictive question can be useful without a mechanistic hypothesis. A pattern is transferable reasoning with conditions and counterexamples, not a title template.

**Dataset knowledge** connects the opportunity to actual observations. It combines inspected data, collection papers/documentation and detailed accounts of previous uses. The host distinguishes a variable's stored name from its measurement meaning, and available rows from independent biological or experimental units. Prior-use work asks what earlier studies actually established, including equivalent questions described with different terms.

Each stream receives independent scrutiny. A fresh questioner reads the substantive accounts and relevant paper notes. Screening can read further sources; each shortlisted question then gets its own planner to examine feasibility, refine the contrast and construct a computation. Independent review follows the whole argument from measurements to interpretation. The [workflow](../WORKFLOW.md) contains the operational roles, artifacts and finite repair allowances.

## Be expansive, then become precise

Early generation can explore scientific connections beyond the most obvious modeling task. Planning narrows those opportunities through affordable checks that could change the decision to invest. A revision that changes the scientific question reopens the affected literature and data checks. When nothing survives, one small additional generation batch may use the failures and untried opportunities within the original budget. A reasoned stop remains a legitimate outcome.

The final document explains the exact question in ordinary language before notation, why an answer matters, the closest precedent, the proposed computation and the interpretations it could support. Review should change scientific decisions when warranted. More documents or reviewer agreement are not evidence of correctness.

## A host-native method

Skills and Markdown references organize the scientific work. Codex or Claude Code supplies planning, code execution, native independent contexts and conversation with the user. Maiwen does not implement a second model transport, execution engine or persistent research state machine. Its records are readable files in the user's workspace. Permissions still come from the host and environment.

## Predecessor

[Maieusis](https://github.com/BeibaiDraco/maieusis) is the predecessor to Maiwen. It established the focus on question formation from successful papers, reusable patterns and dataset-grounded planning. Maiwen retains those scientific aims while moving coordination into the host and making the knowledge, decisions and research plans directly readable. The old project's validation results do not validate this implementation.

The public Maieusis software record is [archived on Zenodo](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21388805). That DOI identifies Maieusis, not Maiwen. See Maiwen's [citation metadata](../CITATION.cff) for this package.
